Frequently Asked Questions

General Questions

Conveyancing is the legal process of transferring ownership of a residential property from a seller to a buyer. In NSW, this involves preparing legal documents, conducting property searches, calculating adjustments (like council rates), and managing the final settlement.
As licensed conveyancers, we specialize 100% in property law. Unlike general solicitors who divide their time between court appearances and family law, our entire focus, training, and daily operations are dedicated exclusively to residential property transfers.

Buying a Residential Property in NSW

Contact us before you sign anything or pay a holding deposit. In NSW, a vendor's agent cannot legally show or market a property without a complete Contract for Sale. We need to review this contract immediately to protect you from unfair terms or hidden property restrictions.
When you buy a residential property in NSW, you automatically get a 5-business-day cooling-off period (unless you buy at auction). During this window, you can pull out of the contract by forfeiting 0.25% of the purchase price to the seller.
This is a certificate signed by us that waives your right to a cooling-off period. Sellers often demand a 66W certificate to secure a quick, unconditional sale, making the contract binding the moment it is exchanged. We will only sign this after conducting all necessary property checks and ensuring your finance is unconditionally approved.

Disbursements are out-of-pocket expenses we pay to third parties on your behalf to complete your purchase. In NSW, these typically include:

  • NSW Land Registry Services (LRS) title searches
  • Council, water, and land tax certificates
  • Pest and building report fees

Selling a Residential Property in NSW

You must have a Contract for Sale drafted and ready before a real estate agent can market your property. To prepare this, we will urgently order required statutory documents, including a zoning certificate (Planning Certificate) from your local NSW council and a title search diagram.
  • Exchange: The buyer and seller sign identical contracts, and the buyer pays the deposit (usually 10%). The deal becomes legally binding.
  • Settlement: Occurs usually 42 days after exchange in NSW. This is when the remaining money changes hands via PEXA, the title transfers, and the buyer gets the keys.

Process & Platforms

PEXA is Australia’s secure Electronic Settlements platform. Property settlements in NSW are mandatory online transactions. We handle the entire PEXA process for you, lodging documents digitally with NSW Land Registry Services and ensuring funds transfer securely and instantly on settlement day.
